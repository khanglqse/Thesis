# Decision log

## 2026-09-07 — preserve and relabel legacy results

Decision: freeze existing CityFlow results as a synthetic OSM-derived benchmark. Reason: routes and arrival flows are generated by random walks and fixed template ratios; no observed count registry feeds the generator. Consequence: old percentages may appear only in a historical-results subsection with their protocol limitations.

## 2026-09-07 — select a two-level empirical design

Decision: use a calibrated one-intersection SUMO pilot for observed Da Nang evidence and the 53-node CityFlow network for scale/generalization. Reason: the public turning table supports one intersection; claiming 53 calibrated intersections would fabricate missing observations. Consequence: contributions are stated separately for empirical fidelity and scalability.

## 2026-09-07 — require independent split and seed axes

Decision: training seed, demand/day seed, simulator seed and evaluation case are separate manifest fields. Validation selects checkpoints/hyperparameters; test is opened only for the final frozen candidates. Reason: the legacy scripts select and report on the same flow and repeat deterministic baselines across learning seeds.

## 2026-09-07 — defense demo is artifact playback

Decision: offline synchronized replay is the primary defense mode; live inference is optional. Reason: the scientific claim comes from aggregate runs, and presentation reliability requires no training, network, package install or WebGL-only path.

## 2026-09-07 — motorcycle sublane safety parameters

Decision: use SUMO sublane resolution 0.4 m, motorcycle `minGapLat=0.30` m and `lcPushy=0.0` for the first pilot. Evidence: on the AM case, seed 1 and a 300-second horizon, the initial `minGapLat=0.15`/`lcPushy=0.5` combination caused 8 collision teleports; changing only pushiness left 1 and only lateral gap left 2; changing both yielded zero. The no-sublane control also yielded zero. Consequence: a regression test requires zero collision teleport warnings. These values pass technical safety smoke but remain calibration parameters, not empirically validated behavior.

## 2026-09-07 — supersede the sublane-primary pilot decision

Decision: use the standard lane model for primary inference and retain sublane
only as sensitivity. Reason: the 300-second gate above did not generalize to a
full hour. Observed-demand full-hour runs produced 1–3 collisions in AM/PM under
sublane and zero in all three regimes under the lane model. Smaller step sizes
and lateral parameter variants did not consistently eliminate collisions.
Consequence: thesis claims will discuss the loss of side-by-side motorcycle
fidelity as a limitation instead of accepting unsafe simulation artifacts.

## 2026-09-07 — isolate the empirically supported junction

Decision: controller training and primary comparison use a 300 m isolated model
of the published target junction; the pinned OSM network is an external stress
reference. Reason: adjacent uncalibrated intersections and sub-meter split edge
fragments caused collisions and insertion bottlenecks unrelated to the target
controller. Bearings remain OSM-derived and lane counts follow the published
site description. Consequence: results support this junction, not the whole
Hai Chau corridor.

## 2026-09-07 — increase motorcycle headway and invalidate prior runs

Decision: set motorcycle Krauss `tau=1.2` seconds for the primary lane model.
Reason: held-out AM demand seed 102 / simulator seed 202 reproduced a rear-end
collision at second 1762 with `tau=1.0`. Changing only `tau` to 1.2 removed it;
an 1800-second regression test now protects that case. Consequence: all tau=1.0
checkpoints and evaluations were moved to a local `superseded` directory, their
comparison was summarized in `results/research_pilot/calibration/tau_sensitivity.json`,
and the full five-seed training/test protocol was rerun with new demand hashes.
### 2026-09-07 — Treat demand scaling as a labeled sensitivity analysis

- **Decision:** Evaluate the frozen published fixed-time controller and all five frozen Dueling DDQN checkpoints at 0.75x, 1.00x, 1.25x, and 1.50x the observed AM camera demand, using five matched departure realizations per level.
- **Reason:** This tests whether the main conclusion survives plausible loading changes without presenting synthetic counts as new observations. The 1.00x level is the observed one-day AM profile; every other level is explicitly labeled synthetic.
- **Statistical unit:** For DDQN, average matched demand realizations within each training seed before computing uncertainty. For fixed time, demand realization is the independent unit.
- **Safety:** No collisions occurred. Four emergency-braking events occurred across the 120 runs and are retained in the report rather than filtered.
- **Consequence:** The controller remains regime dependent: it is worse at 0.75x, indistinguishable at 1.00x, and better at 1.25x and 1.50x. This finding belongs in the thesis and defense; it does not establish performance for unobserved Da Nang intersections.

- **Reason:** The selection rule is independent of the DDQN test advantage and prevents cherry-picking a visually favorable episode.

## 2026-09-08 — Reduce final validation frequency before held-out testing

Decision: keep the predeclared 80 training episodes, but run validation at
episodes 20, 40, 60, and 80 instead of every five episodes. The best checkpoint
is still selected only by the frozen validation set, and the held-out test set
remains unseen until selection is complete.

Reason: the first full-network pilot measured about three minutes per training
episode and about 36 minutes for one validation checkpoint. Validation every
five episodes would spend roughly ten hours per seed re-evaluating nearby
checkpoints, before any final test runs, while epsilon remains above its floor
for roughly the first 28 episodes. Four spaced checkpoints preserve a learning
curve across the exploratory and converged portions of training and make the
ten-seed protocol executable on the available machine.

Consequence: all final seeds and both learned controller families use the same
four checkpoint episodes. This change was recorded before final training and
before examining any held-out test result; pilot data cannot enter the thesis
comparison.

## 2026-09-08 — Correct entry-pending time and unify fixed-time execution

Decision: count outside-network delay only after a vehicle's requested
departure time. Track future vehicles separately as `unreleased`, with the
identities `scheduled = due + unreleased`, `due = inserted + pending`, and
`inserted = arrived + running + removed`. Use the same nominal-duration
fixed-time controller in tuning, validation, and final evaluation.

Reason: the first implementation treated the whole one-hour demand as pending
at second zero, adding roughly 42--67 million controller-independent
vehicle-seconds per validation case. It also rounded non-multiple fixed-time
durations differently between tuning and production evaluation. A CityFlow
probe confirmed that `get_vehicles(True)` contains vehicles whose departure is
due, including those waiting outside an entry lane, while excluding future
departures.

Consequence: the aborted baseline cache and the earlier pilot are diagnostic
only. Baseline tuning and all final learned runs must be regenerated with queue
objective schema 2 before any thesis claim is released.

## 2026-09-08 — Align decision rewards with the one-second queue metric

Decision: compute each DDQN transition reward from the mean local and
downstream queue across every simulator second in its ten-second action
interval. Keep the existing per-lane normalization and switch penalty.

Reason: the previous reward sampled only the final second of each interval, so
two actions with different queue histories could receive the same learning
signal. The primary outcome is sampled every second. Interval averaging gives
the learner the same temporal coverage without multiplying reward scale by ten.
Outside-entry pending remains an evaluation and checkpoint-selection outcome;
it is not assigned to an arbitrary individual signal as a local reward.

Consequence: old pilot checkpoints are incompatible with the final training
objective. Checkpoint contracts now record the reward weights and temporal
aggregation, and all final policies must be trained from scratch.

## 2026-09-08 — Require validation non-inferiority in every demand regime

Decision: checkpoint eligibility now requires validation coverage of exactly
normal, AM peak, and PM peak, with mean normalized queue regret <= 0 in each
regime. Preserve complete completion evidence and the existing maximum
two-percentage-point completion loss per case. Rank eligible checkpoints by
worst regime regret, mean regret, then earlier checkpoint.

Reason: the previous normal-only gate could admit a policy that regressed in
either peak regime, contrary to the requested performance across all three
traffic conditions. This change is recorded before final training or held-out
testing and does not use test outcomes.

Consequence: existing validation rows can be re-scored without simulator runs;
no eligibility is inferred from a successful smoke test. Negative runs retain
their reporting checkpoint. These point-estimate requirements do not establish
statistical non-inferiority; the held-out confidence-interval rules remain
unchanged.

`configs/haichau_training_v2_pilot_epsilon2160.yaml` as a diagnostic-only copy
of `configs/haichau_training_v2.yaml`, changing only
`exploration.epsilon_decay_decisions` from 10,000 to 2,160. Do not change the
canonical config or use a diagnostic checkpoint in the final release.

Controlled comparison: compare `neighbor_phase_ddqn` at learning seed 11 in
the 12-episode `pilot` tier under the canonical config against the diagnostic
config. Both runs must use the same
`results/haichau_network_v2/experiment-manifest.json`, pinned runtime, and
`results/haichau_network_v2/training/final-baseline-tuning.json`. The cached
baseline hash and its six validation-case hashes must match in both run
manifests. Output directories must remain distinct; the diagnostic run should
use `results/haichau_network_v2/training/pilot-epsilon2160-seed11` and must not
overwrite the canonical `pilot-seed11` evidence.

Reason: a full 12-episode pilot contains 4,320 ten-second decisions. With the
canonical 10,000-decision schedule, epsilon remains above its floor throughout
the pilot. A 2,160-decision schedule reaches the same 0.05 floor halfway
through the pilot, permitting a controlled check of whether extended greedy
experience improves the episode-4/8/12 validation trajectory. Compare the
three validation records, scenario-level normalized regret, completion,
replay coverage, optimizer updates, and wall time; do not inspect held-out test
cases.

Consequence: this comparison is pre-final diagnostic evidence only. It cannot

## 2026-09-08 — Respect safety masks in pressure baselines

Decision: raw and hysteretic Max-Pressure choose only valid real green actions.
When the current action is invalid, choose the highest-pressure valid action,
breaking ties by the lowest action index; a singleton mask overrides pressure,
hold time, and switching margin. Hysteretic hold/margin remain in force while
the current action is valid. Missing masks retain the prior unconstrained API;
a supplied mask with no valid real action fails closed.

Reason: the Hai Chau environment enforces maximum red by exposing a forced
mask. Ignoring that mask could repeatedly request an action the environment
rejects and starve a movement. New tests exercise both controllers through a
400-second environment rollout and verify the maximum-red bound.

Consequence: regenerate Hai Chau pressure results with the corrected controllers.
This bug does not invalidate the published single-junction SUMO results:
`SumoSingleJunctionEnv` exposes all three actions as valid and enforces minimum
green internally, with no maximum-red forced mask. Tests confirm pressure
controller decisions are unchanged under that all-valid mask contract.

## 2026-09-08 — Pre-register Hai Chau capacity calibration before final training

Decision: suspend final Hai Chau training at synthetic demand scale 1.0 and run
a validation-only, diagnostic capacity calibration. No test case or test flow
may be opened. Every artifact is marked ineligible for release and test
evaluation. Preparation binds the source experiment manifest, training config,
network, phase contract, source validation flows, code commit, and calibration
code hashes. Existing inputs and completed results are immutable; an identical
manifest may resume incomplete runs, while any hash drift or attempted overwrite
must fail closed.

The first stage runs Fixed-Time with a nominal 30-second green on
`validation_normal_demand31` and `validation_normal_demand32` for 3,600 seconds,
sampling complete network metrics every 300 seconds. It also runs
`validation_normal_demand31` with injection ending at second 3,599 and control
continuing to second 5,400; 7,200 seconds is an explicitly selected diagnostic
extension, not an automatic retry. The second stage scales only flow intervals
at factors 0.25, 0.30, and 0.35, while retaining the validation-normal seed-31
routes, vehicles, three temporal bins, and 500 streams per bin. The third stage
runs the chosen ladder scale or scales on all six validation cases with both
Fixed-Time-30 and the mask-corrected Max-Pressure controller. Only scales from
the registered ladder may enter that stage.

Gates, fixed before any calibration result is generated, are:

1. At every recorded snapshot,
   `scheduled = due + unreleased`, `due = inserted + pending`, and
   `inserted = arrived + running + removed`. All counters are non-negative.
2. At second 3,600, `unreleased = 0` and `removed = 0`.
3. For each normal case and controller, insertion rate is at least 0.95,
   pending/scheduled is at most 0.05, and arrived divided by the number of
   departures that could finish by second 3,600 at route free-flow speed is at
   least 0.70.
4. For the scale-1 seed-31 drain run, completion is at least 0.90 and
   pending/scheduled is at most 0.01 at second 5,400. If the separately chosen
   7,200-second extension is run, its corresponding thresholds are 0.98 and
   0.01. The original 3,600-second snapshot remains reported.
5. In the full six-case stage, every PM-peak run has insertion rate at least
   0.90. For each chosen scale and controller, mean PM-peak queue integral must
   exceed mean normal queue integral and mean PM-peak completion must be below
   mean normal completion. AM peak has no forced ordering because its observed
   temporal profile is back-loaded within the simulation hour.
6. A scale is eligible for pre-final use only if all conservation, normal,
   PM-insertion, and aggregate-ordering gates pass for both validation seeds and
   both controllers. Choose the highest passing registered scale, record and
   freeze it before regenerating any final inputs, and keep the test split sealed.

Free-flow eligibility is computed per scheduled departure from its requested
departure time plus the sum of route-road length divided by the smaller of the
vehicle and lane speed limits. It is a censoring denominator, not a simulated
performance result. Interval scaling preserves each stream's normalized offset
within its temporal bin so lowering demand does not create an artificial burst
at the beginning of each bin.

Reason: scale 1.0 schedules about 25,000 vehicles in the normal hour and routes
more than 4,200 vehicles/hour through a one-lane controlled approach. Its
10--16 percent validation completion is therefore consistent with a synthetic
capacity collapse and cannot be repaired by longer learning alone. The existing
foundation verifier checks provenance, route legality, phase semantics, and a
60-second engine smoke, but it does not establish a feasible one-hour operating
regime.

Consequence: scale 1.0 may remain only as an explicitly oversaturated stress
diagnostic. The thesis network experiment continues to represent synthetic
Hai Chau scalability; capacity calibration does not convert its demand into
observed district-wide traffic counts.

Implementation rule: a passing `stage-d` summary does not authorize a manual
YAML edit. `scripts/apply_haichau_capacity_decision.py` must recompute every
run and scale gate from the completed hash-bound diagnostic artifacts, require
the exact validation/controller matrix, and apply the highest passing registered
candidate. It records `72 / scale` as both an exact fraction and deterministic
decimal. Application requires an explicit `--new-version` and a clean checkout;
the network preparer runs in a temporary tree before the generator config,
regenerated cases, manifest, and immutable decision are installed together. The
bridge may verify its bound validation flows but may not open a test flow.

## 2026-09-08 — Define controlled-signal adjacency by permitted local corridors

Decision: derive the neighbor-DDQN graph from directed CityFlow movements. For
each permitted outgoing road at a controlled signal, traverse only `roadLinks`
with non-empty `laneLinks` through non-signalized intersections. Stop at the
first controlled signal or virtual boundary and retain the shortest reachable
controlled corridor, breaking ties by road count and road identifiers. A
controlled junction is always a boundary, so traversal never connects through
an intermediate signal. Collapse duplicate source--target corridors to one
directed model edge while retaining the exact selected road path, intermediate
intersections, polyline distance, road-hop count, policy, and distribution
statistics in the experiment manifest.

There is no fixed distance or road-hop cutoff. OSM segmentation makes road-hop
counts spatially inconsistent, and a distance cutoff would isolate the sparse
southern two-signal subnetwork. Locality is instead enforced by retaining at
most one nearest controlled destination per physical outgoing road. Exhaustive
enumeration was rejected: it produced 673 edges with median distance 2.72 km,
median 12 road segments, and maximum distance 15.38 km. The selected graph has
154 directed edges, median distance 526 m, 95th-percentile distance 1.37 km,
maximum distance 2.66 km, median two road segments, and 95th-percentile six
segments. It covers all 53 signals with no isolate and has two weak components
(51 signals and the sparse two-signal area). Joining those components would
require an approximately 4.2 km model edge, which is not treated as local
neighbor context.

Reason: the previous direct-road test omitted controlled neighbors separated by
ordinary OSM junction records, leaving 19 of 53 agents isolated in 28 weak
components. Enumerating every reachable first-signal path fixes coverage but
turns remote alternatives into one-hop policy context. The per-outlet nearest
corridor preserves vehicle direction and permitted turns while keeping the
observation graph local and auditable.

Consequence: the phase encoder and defense viewer use the same `RoadNet`
derivation. The foundation verifier rejects any manifest adjacency or canonical
adjacency hash that differs from the roadnet. Existing network learning results
and checkpoints predate this graph and must not be reused as evidence for the
corrected neighbor-DDQN implementation.

## 2026-09-08 — Require demand coverage at every controlled signal

Decision: replace road-count shortest paths with deterministic physical-distance
shortest boundary-to-boundary paths and add only the coverage routes required to
activate the declared controlled area. The canonical contract requires all 53
signals in the route catalog, in each disjoint train/validation/test route-family
partition, and in every generated case. Each signal must occur in at least three
route families so that the three partitions can remain disjoint. A supplemental
route must be a valid, simple boundary-to-boundary path through its target signal,
must not repeat a road or intersection, and may be at most 1.25 times the metric
shortest path for the same origin and destination.

Partitioning and case sampling use deterministic set cover on signal identities
before filling remaining capacity with the existing seeded selection. The
manifest records exact active/inactive signal IDs, route derivation, physical
length, stretch, OD diversity, and coverage for every split and case. Generation
may write the sealed test flows, but calibration, model selection, and training
must not open their contents; final evaluation remains their first use.

Reason: the former single road-hop-shortest route per OD permanently bypassed
four controlled signals in all 30 cases. Calling those nodes active would make a
53-signal control claim false and would give them no traffic-learning signal.
Metric distance also avoids preferring a physically long road merely because OSM
represented it with fewer segments. These rules are fixed before rerunning
capacity calibration or any corrected-network learning experiment.

Consequence: all scale-1, ladder, baseline, pilot, checkpoint, and campaign
artifacts generated with the former route catalog remain diagnostic or
superseded. Capacity calibration restarts on the regenerated foundation before
any final training is authorized.
