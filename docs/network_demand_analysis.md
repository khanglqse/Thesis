# Network & Demand Analysis — Da Nang (Hai Chau District)

*Why the first dense scenario showed almost no benefit from signal control, and how
the network was re-scoped. Written for the thesis defense and the paper.*

## 1. Motivation

After building a virtual Hai Chau district and running a motorbike-heavy ("Vietnamese")
demand, the controllers (DQN, Max-Pressure, Fixed-Time, Random) produced **nearly identical
average travel time** (within 0.4%) and similar total queue (within ~3%), even though the
learned DQN clearly optimized its pressure reward (−2.10 vs Fixed-Time −3.98). This is the
classic "control does not matter" symptom and demanded a root-cause analysis before any
further tuning.

## 2. Real Vietnamese traffic — composition (sanity check)

| Quantity | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Registered motorbikes (Vietnam, end 2023) | ~74.3 million | Vietnam Register (vr.org.vn) |
| Registered cars (Vietnam, end 2023) | ~6.3 million | baogiaothong.vn |
| **Motorbike share of road vehicles** | **~92%** | derived |

Our synthetic flow uses **90% motorbikes**, which matches reality. So the *vehicle mix* was
not the problem.

## 3. Network capacity measurement (the key finding)

Measured directly from the generated CityFlow roadnet of the full Hai Chau bbox
`[108.1888, 16.0213, 108.2360, 16.1037]`:

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Roads (edges) | 11,045 |
| Lanes | 13,150 |
| Total road length | 942.8 km |
| Total lane length | 1,221.5 km |
| Jam capacity (motorbike, 2.5 m footprint) | **~488,600 vehicles** |
| Observed peak vehicles in sim | ~15,000 |
| **Occupancy vs jam capacity** | **~3%** |

**The network was never globally congested.** 15k vehicles is 3% of jam capacity. What
looked like "gridlock" was *local* congestion on the few boundary-entry corridors, while
~97% of the 942 km of road sat empty.

## 4. Root cause — network composition vs. signal coverage

Road types in the imported OSM network:

| Highway type | Edges | Share |
|---|---|---|
| **residential** (alleys/side streets) | **8,534** | **77%** |
| tertiary | 1,025 | 9% |
| secondary | 742 | 7% |
| primary (+link) | 572 | 5% |
| trunk (+link) | 105 | 1% |
| living_street / unclassified / other | ~67 | <1% |

- **89% of roads are single-lane** minor streets.
- Of 4,208 intersections, only **73 are genuinely signal-controlled** (multi-phase). The
  converter attaches a degenerate one-phase "traffic light" to thousands of minor junctions,
  but those exert no control.

Demand was generated by a **random walk over the full road adjacency**, so trips wandered
into the residential alley maze and **mostly never traversed the 73 controllable signals**.
A traffic-signal controller can only influence vehicles that pass its signals; here most
vehicles did not. Hence travel time was dominated by free-flow movement on uncontrolled
streets, and every controller scored almost the same.

## 5. Decision

Re-scope the network to the **arterial grid** — the roads that actually carry through-traffic
and host the signals — which is standard practice in traffic-signal-control research (RESCO,
sumo-rl, Max-Pressure papers all model the arterial network, not every alley).

**Chosen scope (Option 2):** drop `highway.residential` (the 8,534 alleys); keep
trunk / primary / secondary / tertiary (+links) **plus** `unclassified` and `living_street`
for a slightly denser grid. Keep only the largest connected component. Retain the 90%
motorbike mix.

Expected effect: ~2,500 arterial edges instead of 11,045; most remaining junctions are real
arterial signals; demand is forced through controlled intersections, so queues form **at the
signals** and signal control (Max-Pressure, DQN) can demonstrably outperform Fixed-Time.

## 6. Honest modeling caveats (for the thesis)

- CityFlow is lane-disciplined; it approximates but does not reproduce Vietnamese motorbike
  sub-lane filtering / free weaving. The "chaos" is modeled as a dense, tightly-packed,
  motorbike-dominated *lane-based* flow.
- Demand is synthetic (random-walk routes on the arterial network), not calibrated to measured
  Da Nang origin-destination counts.
- Dropping residential streets means the model represents through-traffic on arterials, not
  local access trips — appropriate for evaluating *arterial signal control*, and stated as a
  scoping choice rather than a limitation hidden from the reader.
