# Slide 2: Why Traditional Traffic Control Methods Fail

## Fixed-Time Control
### Problems:
- **Static schedules** based on historical averages
- **Cannot adapt** to real-time conditions
- **Inefficient off-peak**: Unnecessary delays when traffic is light
- **Overwhelmed at peak**: Congestion when demand exceeds capacity

### Real-world impact:
- Wasted time for thousands of commuters daily
- Poor utilization of road capacity
- No response to incidents or special events

## Actuated Control
### Improvements:
- **Responds to vehicle presence** at individual intersections
- **Dynamic timing** based on detector data

### Limitations:
- **Limited coordination** between intersections
- **Rule-based logic** cannot handle complex patterns
- **Sensor dependent** - fails with detection problems
- **Local optimization** may create downstream issues

## Adaptive Systems (SCOOT/SCATS)
### Advanced features:
- **Model-driven optimization**
- **Network-wide coordination**
- **Real-time adjustments**

### Still limited by:
- **Extensive calibration** requirements
- **Computational complexity**
- **Model assumptions** that break in real traffic
- **Limited learning** from experience

## The Fundamental Gap

**Traditional approaches rely on pre-defined models** that cannot:
- Learn from traffic patterns
- Adapt to unpredictable events
- Handle mixed traffic behaviors
- Scale to large networks efficiently

**Result**: Persistent congestion, suboptimal performance, high costs
